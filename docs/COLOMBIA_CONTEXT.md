# Colombia prototype: real context, synthetic practice

Research checked 3 October 2026. The team selected Colombia subject to finding a suitable published real-life story. The following sources meet that condition for a family coffee-tourism setting. They do not constitute interviews, product validation, or endorsement.

## Recommended story: Ana and Eugenio, Finca La Candelaria, Minca

In the farm's [first-person history](https://fincalacandelariaminca.com/historia/), Eugenio describes continuing a family tradition of coffee and cacao farming in the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta. His account connects maintaining the farm with preserving that tradition and respect for the land.

Neighboring tourism operator [Mundo Nuevo's coffee-tour page](https://mundonuevo.com.co/en/tours/tour-de-cafe/) identifies Ana and Eugenio by name and describes visitors learning about artisanal coffee harvesting and processing at La Candelaria. These are two different accounts supporting the same concrete setting: a family farm that also welcomes visitors.

Use the family's story as attributed background in the pitch. Do not use their names for the app's fictional operator, impersonate them, copy their photographs, or imply they use or endorse Welcome. The farm history page contains unrelated template placeholders and testimonials; those are excluded from our evidence. No publication date is established for the relevant history text.

## What this supports—and what remains a hypothesis

| Claim | Evidence / status |
|---|---|
| Colombian family farmers also host visitor experiences | Supported by the two accounts above. |
| Visitors need to understand an experience's timing, activities and arrangements | Product-design inference from the tour workflow; not an interview finding. |
| This particular family struggles with English, rude replies, confusing directions, reviews or connectivity | **Not established. Do not claim it.** |
| Rural connectivity constraints make offline access relevant in Colombia | Separate national evidence: [DANE ENTIC Hogares 2024](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/ENTIC/bol-ENTICHogares-2024.pdf), published 2025, reports Internet access in 41.9% of households in populated rural centers/dispersed rural areas versus 72.5% in municipal seats. Household access is not individual phone ownership, RAM, or this farm's connectivity. |
| Our prototype improves learning or business income | Not evaluated. Automated software checks and model examples do not establish this. |

## Suggested submission framing

“In Minca, Colombia, Ana and Eugenio share their family's coffee-growing knowledge with visitors at Finca La Candelaria. Their published story illustrates how farming and hosting can coexist. Welcome explores a related challenge: helping small tourism operators practice guest conversations and turn visitor feedback into their next lesson, even without an Internet connection. Our demonstration uses a fictional farm and synthetic reviews; it is not a deployment with this family.”

Sources should appear beside the story on the slide or in the video description. Say **informed by a published operator account**, not **co-designed with Colombian operators** or **based on our fieldwork**.

## Prototype decisions

- Colombia is the initial country context; Spanish coaching and English/Spanish guest conversations are the language scope. No regional dialect competence is claimed.
- Keep the app operator unnamed and label exercise facts/reviews as synthetic. Existing durations, meeting points, menus, prices and access arrangements are not La Candelaria's facts.
- Rehearse the work: explain the visit, clarify an ambiguous request, respond respectfully to a dissatisfied guest, and learn from review evidence.
- A complaint about communication can lead to rehearsal. A broken facility needs an operational decision; coaching must not pretend to fix it.
- Keep the real story in submission/research materials, rather than adding promotional narrative to the low-text learner interface.
- Before claiming adoption or impact, seek operator feedback and independent assessment. That later study is separate from this prototype submission.

## Corroborating sector example

[ProColombia's Colombia Travel account](https://colombia.travel/en/blog/discover-immersive-experiences-behind-cup-colombian-coffee) describes Finca del Café in Risaralda developing a boutique hotel and coffee experience from a family farm. This corroborates the broader farming-to-hosting pattern; it does not establish communication deficits or a lack of Internet access.
