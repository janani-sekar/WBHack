# Evidence and lived-experience validation

## What the supplied brief establishes
Noor is fictional. Her household has a calls/messages/mobile-money phone and a daughter's smartphone available mainly on weekends. She buys 3G bundles, has no home Wi-Fi, and receives six or seven farm visitors monthly through word of mouth. Tourism Annex C permits one meaningful workflow, including communication, shaping experiences, and learning from feedback. A training coach must connect practice to one of these real business tasks.

The brief does not establish a particular phone model, language, or country for Noor. It does not require a personal hardship narrative. It does require a relevant problem and evidence.

## Useful primary sources
- Community Homestay Network: account of Poonam translating for women hosts; organization-reported experience, not independent causal evidence. https://communityhomestay.com/impact
- World Bank, Indonesia, March 19, 2025: hospitality/digital marketing support and entrepreneur Indah's experience. https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2025/03/19/indonesia-integrated-tourism-improving-livelihoods-for-thousands-in-lake-toba-and-lombok
- ILO, September 26, 2025: tourism communication, management, digital and green skills needs. https://ilostat.ilo.org/blog/tourism-jobs-in-the-aftermath-of-the-pandemic-skills-and-labour-in-short-supply/
- GSMA Mobile Gender Gap 2025 (2024 data): smartphone/mobile internet inequality; aggregate evidence, not the device inventory of tourism workers. https://www.gsma.com/gender-gap-2025/
- India official CMS Telecom 2025: household smartphone access; household ownership does not imply individual access. https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/Final_press%20release_CMS_T.pdf

These sources motivate investigation; they do not prove this product's efficacy.

## 15-minute operator interview
1. Tell us about the last guest conversation that was difficult.
2. What exactly did the guest ask? What did you do? Who helped?
3. Was the obstacle language, missing information, confidence, or something else?
4. Which phrases do you naturally use? Which language/script do you prefer to read or hear?
5. What phone can you use, when, and with whose help? Check model, OS and free storage with permission.
6. What does bad connectivity prevent? What can you still do?
7. How do you currently learn hospitality skills? Show an existing lesson or workaround if comfortable.
8. Would you prefer a phrasebook, practice with a person, or an app? Why?
9. What would make automated feedback feel wrong or disrespectful?
10. May we use an anonymized account of this incident and test a short prototype with you?

Do not record or publish people without consent. Keep names/contact details/recordings outside the repository. Never rewrite a published story as the team's own lived experience.
