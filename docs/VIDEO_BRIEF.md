## How this follows the video guidelines

The supplied World Bank brief, p.10, asks for an outcome-focused problem statement, evidence, AI value compared with simpler tools, guardrails, an end-to-end demonstration, fit within the user's day, the technical stack and a perspective on localization. Annex C, p.19, identifies a specific gap: small operators may know guests leave happy without knowing which parts of the experience to develop or how to act on feedback. It also warns that basic digital visibility may be the first constraint. Our product addresses the feedback-to-practice step; it does not replace a listing, infrastructure or market access.

**Narrative:** existing local expertise → work involved in hosting → feedback that is difficult to act on → focused practice → a more actionable response before the next visitor. The potential livelihood benefit is a hypothesis; the video demonstrates the workflow that could support it. Make the institutional connection explicit: development organizations already provide human-led hospitality training; this prototype explores AI-delivered practice and feedback that could complement that support.

## 1:15–1:35 — Name the unmet need, then introduce the prototype

**Show:** A clearly labeled synthetic review: “We loved the coffee tasting. The directions did not tell us which gate to use.” Then reveal the app on the phone: “guest-i-mate — Host with Confidence.”

> guest-i-mate brings a focused part of that support into an AI coach: guest-conversation practice and feedback. An operator can use their own reviews to choose a lesson and rehearse before the next visitor, without waiting for a scheduled session. We propose it as a companion to human-led training. Here is that workflow with a fictional farm and synthetic reviews.

## 2:15–2:55 — Connect practice to the next visitor

**Show:** Approve & practice → exercise facts → Spanish coaching / English guest → host reply → generated follow-up → feedback and final recap. Use the actual guest question, not a staged replacement.

> The simulated guest asks about arriving at the farm. The host answers, and the guest follows up based on that reply. Coaching checks confirmed facts, clarity, a useful next step and respectful tone. The host can practice an English conversation while reading feedback in Spanish. This uses hospitality competency guidance and an activity-based approach: practice a task, examine the feedback, then apply it. The operator decides which feedback is useful; learning gains have not yet been evaluated.

**Sources:** [ILO Tourism Regional Model Competency Standard](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@asia/@ro-bangkok/documents/publication/wcms_bk_pb_233_en.pdf), C8/C10, 2006; [ILO Good Practices Guide for Guesthouses and Small Hotels](https://www.ilo.org/publications/good-practices-guide-guesthouses-and-small-hotels), 2012, Lao PDR. ## 2:55–3:25 — Make personalization concrete

**Show:** Save an assessment that matches the reply → Progress → What the coach remembers → source quote and suggested focus. Show the withdrawal control. Do not promise a particular next recommendation unless the recorded result demonstrates it.

> In Progress, the host can see what was saved and why. Accepted results and language preferences inform future practice. Uncertain or rejected assessments do not count, and the host can stop using saved feedback. This is local memory, not retraining the model. A phrasebook provides familiar phrases; this workflow connects a business's feedback with a conversation the operator can rehearse and review.

**Why not a simpler tool? (on-screen table, ~10 s, during the Progress shot):**

| | Phrasebook / checklist | guest-i-mate |
|---|---|---|
| Uses this host's own reviews | No | Groups reviews into themes and quotes the words behind each one |
| Practice | Read and memorize fixed phrases | Generated guest asks follow-ups based on what the host actually said |
| Feedback | Self-check | Five criteria (answers the request, facts, unknowns, next step, tone), with uncertain grades left unscored |
| Adapts | Same for everyone | Accepted results and language choices shape the next exercise |
| Language | One fixed translation | Spanish coaching with an English or Spanish guest, switchable per session |
| Works offline, costs nothing to run | Yes | Yes — local model, no cloud |

> A phrasebook tells you what to say. It can't read your guests' reviews, ask you the follow-up a real guest would ask, or tell you that you forgot the meeting point. That is where a small local model earns its place.

This is a design comparison, not a measured result; do not attach percentages to it.

## Recording emphasis

- State the analogy in plain language: a trainer offers practice, feedback and follow-up; our AI offers a bounded version of those interactions. It does not provide certification, workplace supervision, human judgment or the wider support of a foundation.
- A training foundation or cooperative is a potential distribution partner, not an existing partnership. We have not verified hospitality-specific programming at the exact Srishti Foundation website, so do not substitute its name for the verified examples above.

- Let the operator's work explain each screen. Say “The host checks what guests struggled with,” rather than “Here is our sentiment-analysis feature.”
- Establish the documented gap before introducing AI. Do not claim that every tourism barrier is a training problem or that this tool brings customers to an unlisted farm.
- A potentially better guest experience is the proposed route to business value. More bookings, referrals or income are outcomes to measure, not achievements to announce.
- Keep the named family's published experience separate from the World Bank's fictional Noor and our authored review examples. No invented quotes, user interviews or testimonials.
- If shortening the video, trim stack details and navigation pauses first. Preserve the problem, one end-to-end workflow, evidence scope and localization statement.

## Exact demonstration inputs and actions

1. Use the built-in three synthetic reviews. They are authored examples, not customer research. Do not add the real farm's name to them.
2. Set review explanations to Spanish. Confirm the arrival finding has two distinct review sources. If the model gets a detail wrong, show the limitation or fix and re-evaluate the implementation before recording; do not replace output with hand-written text.
3. Approve the arrival communication finding. The current mapping selects the `no_map` exercise and generates a new opening. Its facts are: a farm entrance beside a blue gate, a cooperative building, and the entrance opposite that building. They are fictional exercise facts.
4. First example reply: “Meet us at the farm entrance.” This deliberately omits landmarks. The actual follow-up and grading are model-generated, so do not script a guaranteed score.
5. A more complete response, when relevant to the generated guest question: “The entrance is beside the blue gate, opposite the cooperative building. Can you see that building?” Answer the actual follow-up if it asks something different. Do not invent road conditions, opening times or availability.
6. Complete the third reply. Review the recap. Save a non-uncertain assessment only if it matches what was said. Open Progress and show its actual source. Do not call same-session improvement retention or independent evidence of learning.
7. Capture all inference waits in the original recording. In the edited video, shorten them transparently. Use captions and readable zooms rather than speeding through small text. Aim for 4:50 and check the exported duration stays below 5:00.
8. Check the shared profile for unrelated prior practice. Use a separate recording database if a clean history is needed; preserve existing demo data. Do not silently reset user history.
